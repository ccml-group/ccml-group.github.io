# Research figure provenance

Updated 20 October 2026 (published ML figure revision). The four overview images are stored in `assets/research/` and used on Home and Research. Research also links to each full-size image. These are 1600 × 1600 PNG collages of existing publication panels with explanatory workflows and a published ML method illustration. Published atomic structures and numerical results remain original source panels, not generative recreations.

Panels were cropped, resized proportionally and placed on white canvases. Old panel letters and circled panel indices were removed from their blank margins. Existing atomic structures, scientific curves, chemical labels and reaction arrows were retained. New headings and explanatory visuals communicate the role of learning and prediction. The Functional Materials Design overview uses the published ALIGNN Fig. 1 in place of the rejected generated ML illustration. Sources are recorded here rather than printed inside the overview images; the externally sourced ALIGNN panel has a short plain-text credit beneath the figure on the site, with no outbound source link.

The Fe₄N DOS panels show hydrogen at surface (upper) and bulk (lower) octahedral sites. The RuO₂ Wulff shapes are examples at different oxygen chemical potentials, not a kinetic time sequence. The separate material examples in a collage do not imply a single combined material or experiment.

The active-learning workflow is a schematic of selecting new first-principles training data during structure exploration. The interface workflow explicitly separates ML-guided surface exploration from subsequent DFT refinement and interface simulation; the aqueous reaction panels are not relabeled as ML trajectories. The catalyst graphic shows the published data-driven hybrid model without asserting a neural-network architecture.

The PI confirmed on 20 October 2026 that ML property prediction and candidate exploration are current research activities in functional materials design. The external ALIGNN panel illustrates one published way of representing atomic and bond-angle information for property prediction. It does not imply that ALIGNN was developed by CCML, that CCML uses this particular architecture, or that the older WO₃/Fe₄N panels are ML outputs. The source figure is reproduced in full, resized proportionally and placed above the four existing paper panels. No generated content, numerical predictions or performance claims were added.
