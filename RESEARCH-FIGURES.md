# Research figure provenance

Updated 20 October 2026. The four overview images are stored in `assets/research/` and used on Home and Research. Research also links to each full-size image. These are 1600 × 1600 PNG collages of existing publication panels, not AI-generated atomic structures or simulated results.

Panels were cropped, resized proportionally and placed on white canvases. Old panel letters and circled panel indices were removed from their blank margins. Existing atomic structures, scientific curves, chemical labels and reaction arrows were retained. New headings describe the groups of panels. Sources are recorded here rather than printed inside the overview images.

| Overview | Original papers and panels |
| --- | --- |
| Machine-Learning Atomistic Simulation | Universal ML potential training schematic, DOI [10.1016/j.commatsci.2026.114666](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.commatsci.2026.114666), graphical abstract; staged RuO₂ surface exploration, DOI [10.1021/acs.jpcc.3c04049](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpcc.3c04049), Fig. 3 right-hand structures and Fig. 4 lower-row Wulff morphologies. |
| Dynamic Surfaces & Interfaces | Reconstructed BiVO₄ aqueous interfaces, DOI [10.1021/jacs.4c17739](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.4c17739), Fig. 3 first-column direct/indirect dissociation snapshots and Fig. 4 coordination and interfacial mechanism schematics. |

The Fe₄N DOS panels show hydrogen at surface (upper) and bulk (lower) octahedral sites. The RuO₂ Wulff shapes are examples at different oxygen chemical potentials, not a kinetic time sequence. The separate material examples in a collage do not imply a single combined material or experiment.

Publisher full-text/image access was checked on the user's authorized MacBook browser. New source images were obtained through publisher figure views; previously saved local graphical abstracts were reused. Original publications retain their respective authorship and copyright. This document records provenance and edits; access alone is not a reuse license.

Some original raster panels are relatively small; their native detail limits enlargement. No generative upscaling or scientific redrawing was used in the final assets. The previous artistic illustrations remain in `assets/` for recovery but are no longer displayed on Home or Research.
