# Research figure provenance

Updated 27 October 2026 (functional-materials panels aligned to the linked papers). The four overview images are stored in `assets/research/` and used on Home and Research. Research also links to each full-size image. These are 1600 × 1600 PNG collages of existing publication panels with concise headings and a published ML method illustration. Published atomic structures and numerical results remain original source panels, not generative recreations.

Panels were cropped, resized proportionally and placed on white canvases. Old panel letters and redundant snapshot indices were removed from blank margins. Scientific structure identifiers linking curves to their atomic models remain intact. Existing atomic structures, scientific curves, chemical labels and reaction arrows were retained. New headings communicate the role of learning and prediction. The first two overviews now use original research results instead of the added three-box workflows. The Functional Materials Design overview uses the published ALIGNN Fig. 1 in place of the rejected generated ML illustration. Sources are recorded here rather than printed inside the overview images; the externally sourced ALIGNN panel has a short plain-text credit beneath the figure on the site, with no outbound source link.

The functional-materials figure now uses the same two CCML studies as its representative-paper links (#2 and #21). The VSe₂ crop is (266, 0, 520, 269) in the 520 × 269 original: all three material comparisons, labels, hydrogen symbols and oxide layers are preserved; the separate photoelectron-spectroscopy instrument illustration is omitted. Fe₄N panels are no longer part of this overview. The separate material examples do not imply a single combined material or experiment.

The atomistic overview pairs the published training scheme with an actual Au–Au interaction benchmark and ML-guided TiO₂ structure exploration. These are distinct studies: the Au fine-tuning model is not represented as the model used for the TiO₂ results. The figure retains graph axes, units, legends, curves, structure identifiers and compositions. The original PDF image transparency was composited onto white using its embedded soft mask before cropping; scientific pixels were not redrawn.

The interface overview separates experimental TiO₂ reconstruction from BiVO₄ aqueous-interface simulations. In the TiO₂ micrographs, only the upper background strip containing panel letters and timestamps was cropped. Source times (0, 28 and 34 s) are repeated below each image. Atomic contrast, red arrows, green position guides and 1 nm scale bars are preserved. The BiVO₄ direct and indirect reaction snapshots have equal display sizes. Coordination-state and water-activation diagrams remain unchanged in scientific content; the aqueous panels are not relabeled as ML trajectories. The catalyst graphic shows the published data-driven hybrid model without asserting a neural-network architecture.

The PI confirmed on 20 October 2026 that ML property prediction and candidate exploration are current research activities in functional materials design. The external ALIGNN panel illustrates one published way of representing atomic and bond-angle information for property prediction. It does not imply that ALIGNN was developed by CCML, that CCML uses this particular architecture, or that the WO₃/VSe₂ publication panels are ML outputs. The source figure is reproduced in full and resized proportionally within a balanced two-by-two layout. The upper row pairs the tungsten-bronze atomic framework with the VSe₂ surface-oxidation comparison. Optical selectivity sits at lower left and the wide ALIGNN panel spans the lower right. The two CCML studies have distinct headings, and the ML reference remains a separate method illustration. All aspect ratios and source contents are preserved. No generated content, numerical predictions or performance claims were added.
